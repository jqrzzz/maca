0The big picture: two tracks

Keep two questions separate. They follow different laws and different offices.

Track 1, the organization. Can we register a Thai foundation for this mission, and on what terms? This is the Civil and Commercial Code and the Ministry of Interior.
Track 2, the person. What may the Korean doctor lawfully do here? This splits again: governing or running the foundation (a work-permit question) versus personally treating patients (a medical-license question, and the harder one).
The one thing to internalize: the doctor does not need a Thai medical license to found, fund, govern, or run the foundation, or to lead education, training, and program design. He does need one to personally diagnose or treat patients in Thailand. Design the role around that line and most of the difficulty disappears.

ARegister the foundation

Legal basis: Civil and Commercial Code, Sections 110 to 136, and the Ministerial Regulations on the Registration of Foundations B.E. 2545 (2002). Filed through the District Office where the foundation will sit, then approved by the Ministry of Interior.

The filing process

On foreign control. Some advisors flag foreign-ownership limits (a figure of 49 percent of assets is sometimes cited) and extra scrutiny of foreign-led nonprofits. BThe Korean doctor

Three layers, from easiest to hardest: taking a seat on the board, working here for the foundation, and personally practicing medicine.

As a founder, committee member, or advisor

To work for the foundation in Thailand

Unpaid does not mean exempt. Thai law treats almost any work, paid or not, as “work.” Performing duties for the foundation inside Thailand, even as a volunteer director, needs a work permit and the right visa. Working without one risks heavy fines (5,000 to 100,000 THB), up to five years in prison, deportation, and a ban.

To personally practice medicine

This is the hard barrier. A foreign medical degree and a Korean license do not allow treating patients in Thailand. To practice, the doctor must register with the Medical Council of Thailand and pass all three parts of the Thai national licensing exam. Parts 1 and 2 are in English; Part 3 is in Thai, so working Thai is effectively required.

A foundation is an ongoing obligation, not a one-time filing.
