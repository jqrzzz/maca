# Fiscal-sponsor outreach pack

_The concrete next move from the entity memo
([`entity-options.md`](entity-options.md)): approach a US fiscal sponsor so US
donors get 501(c)(3) tax-deductibility now, with no entity to form. This pack is
**ready-to-send drafts plus the due-diligence kit** — grounded only in verified
facts, with `[fill in]` slots for the things only the founder knows. Review,
fill, send. Nothing here overclaims: PRASM is presented exactly as what it is._

## Who to approach (from the research)

Work a short list in parallel rather than one at a time:

1. **Give2Asia** — US 501(c)(3) built for US donors funding Asian projects;
   deep Thailand experience. May expect more track record; ask anyway —
   worst case they tell you what they'd need to see.
2. **Players Philanthropy Fund (PPF)** — comprehensive (Model A) sponsor;
   states 600+ projects across 35 countries and global relief experience.
3. **Two or three more from the
   [Fiscal Sponsor Directory](https://fiscalsponsordirectory.org/?page_id=1507)
   (filter: International Development)** — compare fees and cross-border
   experience before signing anything.

## The intro email (short — this is the door-opener)

> **Subject:** Fiscal sponsorship inquiry — medical care & identity records for
> Kayan refugees in northern Thailand
>
> Hello [name / sponsorship team],
>
> I'm [founder name], a medical doctor and the founder of PRASM, a small
> initiative supporting Kayan refugee families from Myanmar who live off-grid
> and largely undocumented in the hills of Mae Hong Son, Thailand. We cover
> urgent medical care and transport, support education access, back the
> village's solar and water systems, and build a written medical history for
> each patient — which, for people with no papers, becomes a first proof of
> identity.
>
> We're an emerging, founder-led project — not yet a registered entity — with

> looking for a fiscal sponsor so that US donors can give tax-deductibly while

>
> Would PRASM be a fit for your program? We have a public website, a published
> policy suite (safeguarding, code of conduct, complaints, conflict of
> interest, and a funds-protection policy written for our cross-border
> context), and we can provide a project budget and references.
>
> Thank you — happy to share anything else you need.
>
> [founder name]
> [email] · [site URL]

## The project summary (attach or paste when they ask)

> **PRASM — dignity, identity, and care for the stateless.**
>
> PRASM supports Kayan refugees from Myanmar who fled war and now live,
> largely undocumented and off-grid, in the hills of Mae Hong Son, Thailand.
> Without Thai ID, families are priced out of hospitals (charged full foreigner
> rates, uninsured), children are barred from public school, and many people —
> especially children — have no identity document at all.
>
> Founded by a medical doctor, PRASM works in the community's own rhythm:
> covering urgent treatment and the journey to reach it; supporting learning in
> the village; maintaining and extending the solar, water, and food systems
> that keep the village self-reliant; and building a hand-written medical
> record for each patient. These records guide future care — and, over time,
> become quiet proof that a person exists, the seed of our planned identity and
> digital-records programs.
>
> PRASM is an emerging initiative, honest about its stage: founder-led, not yet
> registered, with a published governance and policy suite and a commitment to
> account for any gift on request. Sponsorship would let our US supporters give
> tax-deductibly while we complete our structure in Thailand.
>
> **Funding needs:** [fill in — a simple 12-month budget]

## Questions to ask THEM (from the entity memo)

1. Do you sponsor a **Thailand-based, foreign-led humanitarian project**, and
   how do you handle **cross-border disbursement** (method, timing, wire fees)?
2. **Fee** — percentage, what it covers, any minimums or setup costs.
3. Do you accept **crypto** and **in-kind** gifts?
4. Do you require a **US-based responsible person**?
5. Model A (comprehensive) vs Model C (pre-approved grant) — which do you offer,
   and what control do we keep over the project and its name?
6. What does **offboarding** look like when we later form our own entity —
   do funds and donor relationships transfer cleanly?
7. Given our context (conflict-affected border, some undocumented
   beneficiaries), what **AML/sanctions due diligence** will you run, and what
   reporting will you need from us? (Our funds-protection policy is the
   starting answer.)
