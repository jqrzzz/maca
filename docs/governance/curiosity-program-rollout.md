## How to read this, and one honesty note

This pack turns the proposal into things a person can pick up and run: a
recommended pilot, the gates to clear first, a consent script, and the exact
policy wording to publish on launch.

The honesty note matters. The public Safeguarding, Privacy, and Responsible AI
policies are **not edited yet**. They describe what PRASM does today, and today
the Curiosity Program is a plan, not a running program. The draft policy language
below is held here, ready to publish the day the pilot starts, so the public
record becomes accurate at exactly the moment the program becomes real, and not
before. AI proposes; the founder publishes.

## What is settled, and what is still to set

Settled with the founder (June 2026):

1. **Curiosity now, registry later.** Run the low-risk literacy work first; the
   Tier 2 registry waits for a secure backend (Stage B/C).
2. **Open and consented, not "from a distance."** We learn what sparks a child
   openly, with the family's knowledge, and they can opt out.
3. **A role, not a bounty.** The steward is paid a stipend for the role plus
   costs, with no per-profile, per-signup, or per-photo element.

Still to set:

- **The stipend figure**, benchmarked to local community-health-worker norms.
  Not invented here; set it with Ong and a local sanity check.
- **Pilot size and location logistics.** Delegated to the build; the
  recommendation is below, and it is the founder's to adjust.
- **Kid-mode AI tooling.** Which constrained, supervised model or app the
  children actually use (a narrowed general model, or a local one). A
  near-term decision, not a blocker for design and consent work.

## Recommended first pilot

A recommendation, not a rule. Start small enough to supervise well and to learn
honestly.

- **People.** About 6 to 10 consenting families, roughly 8 to 15 learners across
  children, teens, and a few adults. Small on purpose.
- **Place.** One common space the community already trusts, with one or two
  shared, durable devices and a way to charge them (solar). Not a device per
  child.
- **Rhythm.** Weekly supervised sessions led by the steward, with more than one
  trusted adult present, over about 8 to 12 weeks.
- **Then review.** Hold against the honest metrics below before growing. Growth is
  earned by the pilot working, exactly as the blueprint's "each stage earns the
  next" rule asks.

The point of starting this small is not caution for its own sake. It is that a
small pilot can be supervised properly, consent can be real and unhurried, and we
can tell the truth about whether children come back.

## Pilot readiness checklist

No child is enrolled until these are real.

- [ ] **Steward onboarding complete.** Code of Conduct, data and confidentiality
      agreement, conflict-of-interest, and safeguarding clearance recorded, the
      same `worksWithVulnerable` gate the roster already tracks
      ([`../../scripts/team/schema.ts`](../../scripts/team/schema.ts)).
- [ ] **Kid-mode safety bar met** (see below). No child uses AI before it is.
- [ ] **Consent script translated** into the family's language by a trusted human
      translator, and reviewed. Do not auto-translate sensitive consent text.
- [ ] **Two trusted adults** present at every session, per Safeguarding.
- [ ] **Stipend agreed** with the steward, in writing, as a role, with the
      no-per-head principle stated plainly.
- [ ] **Spark-fund budget line opened** under `education` (and `sustainable`
      where it fits), with the founder as the approver of each spark.
- [ ] **Offline capture** set up: sessions and any records work without signal and
      sync later.
- [ ] **A named adult is accountable** for the AI tool, per the Responsible AI
      policy.
- [ ] **The complaints channel** is explained to families, in their language.
- [ ] **Draft policy language published** (Safeguarding and Responsible AI
      additions below) on the day the pilot starts.

## Kid-mode safety bar

The concrete minimum before any child uses AI:

- **Constrained mode.** Age-appropriate content, safety filtering, a scope
  narrowed toward learning and wonder, in the child's language where possible.
- **Supervised and shared.** More than one trusted adult; never a child alone with
  a device.
- **Offline-tolerant.** A dropped signal degrades gracefully; it never fails a
  curious child mid-question.
- **Sessions are Tier 2.** A child's words are used to help that child learn and,
  with the family's knowledge, to understand what sparks them. They are never sent
  to a general-purpose model as training data, and never quoted publicly without
  consent.
- **Named accountable adult** for the tool, as the Responsible AI policy requires.

## Guardian consent script (English master)

A plain, warm script the steward reads and explains to a parent or guardian, in
their own language. This English version is the master; it must be translated by a
trusted human translator and reviewed before use.

> Hello. Thank you for your time. I would like to invite your child to a small
> learning club. I want to explain it clearly, and you are free to say no. Saying
> no changes nothing else between your family and us.
>
> **What it is.** Your child can sit with a friendly, safe helper on a tablet and
> ask questions about anything they wonder about, plants, animals, the sky,
> numbers, stories. A grown-up is always there with them. It is for learning and
> curiosity.
>
> **What we keep.** To begin, only a name or a nickname your child likes, and
> their age group. Nothing more, unless there is a clear reason and your
> agreement.
>
> **A gift.** If you would like, we can print and frame a photo of your child or
> your family for you to keep. That is yours. We only keep a copy if you say it is
> alright.
>
> **What we notice.** When your child keeps getting excited about something, we
> notice it, openly, so we can bring something helpful next time, like a few seeds
> and a book for a child who loves plants. We will always tell you what we
> noticed. You can ask us to stop noticing at any time.
>
> **It is free and voluntary.** There is no payment for taking part, and nothing
> your child receives depends on sharing anything private. Your child can stop any
> time, and you can ask us to remove their record any time, and we will.
>
> **If you have a worry.** You can tell me, or contact PRASM directly, any time.
> We take every concern seriously.
>
> May I welcome your child to the club?

## Recording consent (keep it minimal)

The steward records only:

- that a parent or guardian gave consent, and their relationship to the child,
- the date, and the language the script was given in,
- whether the family wants the printed photo gift,
- nothing about the conversation beyond what the celebratory layer holds.

Consent is revocable. If a family withdraws, the record is removed.

## Draft policy language, to publish on launch

DRAFT. Add these only when the pilot goes live, so public copy stays accurate.
Both are written in the public house voice (dignity first, no overclaim).

### Addition to the Safeguarding policy

A new section, "Incentives, gifts, and our learning programs":

> When we run learning programs, any support we offer is set and governed by
> PRASM, applied openly and consistently, and never in the gift of an individual.
> No one acting for PRASM offers a child money, gifts, or help in exchange for
> anything. Taking part is always voluntary, and a family keeps any welcome gift
> or help whether or not their child continues. We recognize children with
> encouragement, never cash, and we never make any support depend on sharing
> private information. People who run sessions are supported as workers, with a
> stipend for the role, not a payment for each child who takes part.

### Addition to the Responsible AI policy

A new section, "Children and AI":

> Where children use AI in our programs, they do so in a safe, age-appropriate
> mode, with a trusted adult present, and never alone. A child's conversations are
> treated as sensitive (Tier 2) data: used to help that child learn and, with the
> family's knowledge, to understand what interests them, never used to train a
> general-purpose model, and never shared without consent. A named adult is
> accountable for the tool, and it is built to work offline and to fail safe.

## What success looks like

Briefly, and in full in the proposal. We watch return rate (learners who come
back across several weeks), depth (real questions, topics explored), follow-
through that actually helped, consent health (freely given, and families feeling
free to withdraw), and zero safeguarding incidents. We never report a head count
as if it were impact.
