# Entity & legal-structure options — decision memo

_Research for the founder, June 2026. **This is research, not legal advice** —
confirm everything with a Thai nonprofit lawyer (and, for US donors, a US
nonprofit attorney) before acting. Method note: compiled from web search of
reputable law/tax-firm and government sources; primary pages couldn't be opened
directly in this environment, so figures that **vary by source are flagged**.
Sources are listed at the end._

## The one insight that drives everything

**Donor tax-deductibility is country-specific.** A Thai foundation can (after a
hard, separate approval) make gifts deductible **for Thai taxpayers** — it does
**nothing** for US or other foreign donors. US donors only get a deduction when
money flows through a **US 501(c)(3)**. So "what entity?" depends first on
**where your donors are**, and second on **whether the founder is Thai or
foreign**. The memo branches on those two variables.

## Lead recommendation (most likely scenario)

Assuming **international / mixed donors with a meaningful US share** (your site is
English; you take Stripe, PayPal, Patreon, crypto) and a **possibly-foreign
founder**, the disciplined path is **staged**, not "register a foundation first":

1. **Now (~0–3 months): get a US fiscal sponsor (comprehensive / "Model A").**
   This instantly gives US donors 501(c)(3) tax-deductibility and gives you the
   credibility of an audited US charity behind you — **without forming any
   entity**, for a fee of roughly **5–10%** of funds. It's the fastest unlock for
   both deductibility _and_ grant eligibility.
2. **In parallel (~3–12 months): establish a Thai legal presence to operate.**
   Either the **Foreign Private Organization (FPO) "permission to operate"**
   (faster; for a foreign org operating in Thailand, no Thai company formed) or
   begin a **Thai foundation** (slower, board + fund requirements — recruit the
   board first; see `board-recruitment.md`).
3. **Later (12+ months), if it matters:** pursue **Thai approved-charity status**
   (Section 47(7)) for _Thai_ donor deductibility, and/or your **own US 501(c)(3)
   ("Friends of")** only once volume justifies the cost and admin of leaving the
   sponsor.

Why staged: it buys legitimacy and deductibility in weeks instead of the **12+
months** a Thai foundation can now take, while you build the board and decide the
entity calmly.

### For _receiving_ grants from US foundations (good to know)

US **private foundations** that grant to a non-US org like PRASM must either do an
**equivalency determination** (a qualified practitioner certifies you're
equivalent to a US public charity — heavier, but then no per-grant reporting) or
exercise **expenditure responsibility** (lighter to start, but requires oversight

- reporting). US **public charities** (including a fiscal sponsor or a Friends-of)
  grant abroad under the lighter **"discretion and control" + recordkeeping**
  standard. Net: a US fiscal sponsor smooths this for you too.

## How the recommendation changes by scenario

- **Donors mostly US →** fiscal sponsorship now; own 501(c)(3) later. Thai entity
  is only for _operating legally_, not for deductibility.
- **Donors mostly international (non-US) →** fiscal sponsorship still gives the
  broadest credibility; deductibility matters less in many countries, so weight
  legitimacy + ease.
- **Donors mostly Thai →** flip the order: prioritize the **Thai foundation +
  Section 47(7)** approved-charity status; a US sponsor matters little.
- **Founder is Thai →** the Thai foundation is more straightforward and can come
  sooner; less foreign scrutiny.
- **Founder is foreign →** favor fiscal sponsorship now + **FPO** for Thai
  operations; a Thai foundation will need Thai board members and extra vetting.

## PRASM-specific risks to raise

- **Funds to a conflict-affected border** (crypto, in-kind, Western Union):
  fiscal sponsors and banks will ask about AML / sanctions / no-diversion — your
  `/funds-protection` policy already states the stance; be ready to evidence it.
- **Crypto:** confirm whether a chosen sponsor accepts and liquidates crypto
  gifts; not all do.
- **Undocumented beneficiaries:** keep beneficiary data out of anything a sponsor
  or registry would publish (your Tier-2 data rule).
- **Tightening Thai rules:** a proposed Act on Associations and Foundations could
  add oversight / foreign-funding scrutiny — a reason to keep the Thai-entity
  decision under a lawyer's eye.

## Checklist to take to a Thai nonprofit lawyer

1. For our objectives, what's the **exact registered-fund minimum** and how much
   in cash? (Sources cite anywhere from ~100k cash up to 500k total.)
2. Can a **foreigner** be founder/committee, and is a **Thai-majority board**
   required in practice? What documents/interviews apply?
3. **Foundation vs FPO vs association** for us — realistic **timeline and total
   cost** of each today?
4. Path and **waiting period** to **Section 47(7)** approved-charity status for
   Thai donor deductions (some sources imply a track record is needed — confirm).
5. Reporting/audit obligations once registered; the **e-Donation** system (now
   required from 2026 for Thai deductibility).
6. Any constraints on **receiving foreign funding** for border/refugee work.

## Checklist for a prospective US fiscal sponsor

1. Do you sponsor a **Thailand-based, foreign-led humanitarian project**, and how
   do you handle **cross-border disbursement**?
2. **Fee %**, what it covers, wire fees, and any minimums.
3. Do you accept and process **crypto** and **in-kind** gifts?
4. Do you require a **US-based responsible person**? (Several sponsors do.)
5. Model A (comprehensive) vs Model C (regrant) — which, and what control do we
   keep? Candidates surfaced in research include **Players Philanthropy Fund**
   (states 600+ projects across 35 countries) among others — compare a few.
