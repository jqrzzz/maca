The steward's guide

Curiosity Program

This is the English version. Your language (Thai, Burmese, or Kayan) will be added with your help.

You are the village steward. Here is what this is, why it helps, and what you do. You do not need to be a teacher or know about computers.

What it is

A learning club. Children sit with a friendly, safe helper on a tablet and ask about anything they wonder about. A grown-up is always there.

Why it helps

There is no teacher out here, but children are full of questions. The helper answers them and never gets tired. When a child keeps loving something, we bring them something to grow it.

What you do, each visit

  1. Gather a few children at the common spot.
  2. Make sure another trusted grown-up is there with you.
  3. Hand them the tablet. Let them ask, by typing or by speaking.
  4. Let them hear the answers and keep exploring.
  5. Write one short note: who came, and what excited them.

Three rules to keep

Consent belongs to the family. Always ask first, and they can say no.

A grown-up is always with the children.

Private things about people stay private.

You are supported

This is a real role, with a stipend for the role, not a payment for each child. The helper does the answering. Your job is to make it safe, welcoming, and fun.

Practice a visit, step by step

A calm walk-through of one visit, so you know just what to do. Read it as many times as you like.

1. Get consent first

Gather a few children at the common spot, and make sure another trusted grown-up is with you. Ask each family first, in their language. They are free to say no, and nothing is lost if they do.

What you can say

"This is a free learning club for curious children. Would you like yours to join today?"

A parent agrees first, and a grown-up always stays.

2. Welcome the explorer

Add the child with a name or a nickname and an age group. If the family likes, take a welcome photo to print and frame as a gift.

What you can say

"What name would you like to use here?"

3. Hand over the tablet

Let the child ask anything they wonder about, by typing or by speaking. Turn on read aloud so they can hear the answers. The helper is kind and never grows tired.

What you can say

"Ask anything you are curious about."

4. Notice a spark

When a child keeps lighting up about one thing, the app notices it openly. It will appear later in your Sparks list to suggest a small follow-up.

What you can say

"You really love this. Let us tell the grown-up who helps you, so we can bring you more."

5. Write one short note

Before you finish, write one line: who came, and what excited them. That is all the record we keep.

After the visit

  • You suggest a spark for a child who keeps lighting up.
  • The founder approves it before anything is bought.
  • We bring something small to help them grow it.

For the person introducing this

A short way to walk the steward through it, in his world.

  1. Start with his hands, not a talk
    Let him use kid mode himself first, by voice. Once it answers him, the purpose is obvious.
  2. Explain it in his world
    No teacher out here; curious children get to learn; when we see what a child loves, we bring something to grow it; you are the one who makes it happen.
  3. Keep the job small
    Walk through the five-step weekly routine above. That is the whole job.
  4. Three safety rules
    Consent belongs to the family; a grown-up is always present; private things about people stay private.
  5. Make him the language partner
    Ask for the words in his language. It gives him ownership, and gets us real Kayan, which we never guess.
  6. He does not need to understand the technology
    Aim for confidence, a clear role, and trust, not technical understanding. The tool is just a tool he is good at using.
